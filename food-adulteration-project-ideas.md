# Food Adulteration — AI/ML Internship / Research Project Ideas
### Detecting saffron, honey, milk, spice, oil & staple-food fraud, with cross-verified open datasets

Generated by a fan-out of 6 domain research agents (each web-searched prior research and verified whether the datasets actually exist and are freely accessible). Every idea follows the mentor's flavor: **a concrete region → a real local fraud problem → an AI/ML approach → data that genuinely exists.**

How to read the novelty score: 5 = genuinely fresh framing / under-served niche; 3 = the method is mature, the novelty is in the region/data/application twist. Nothing below scores under 3 — saturated topics (yet-another in-distribution CNN reporting 99% on a single self-collected set) were deliberately dropped in favour of the *real* open gaps: cross-device / cross-instrument generalization, low-concentration quantification, open-set "unseen adulterant" detection, and cheap-sensor deployment.

> ⚠️ **The single most important finding — read before anything else.** In food-adulteration ML, **the data is the hard part, not the model.** Published accuracies of 95–100% are almost always single-instrument, small-*n*, in-distribution numbers that do not transfer. Worse, the most *popular* datasets are traps: the Kaggle **"Predict Purity and Price of Honey"** set is **explicitly synthetic** (fabricated by its uploader), and the Kaggle **"Milk Quality"** set is a small, subjective, provenance-free quality table that is **not an adulteration dataset at all**. Building a credible project on either is a credibility hole. Every dataset below is flagged **verified-open**, **real-but-access-to-confirm**, or **synthetic / avoid**.

**Default tech stack for almost all of these:** Python — `scikit-learn` (PCA, PLS, SVM, RF, pipelines, nested CV), `XGBoost`/`LightGBM`, `PyTorch`+`torchvision` (image/HSI CNNs, 1D-CNNs on spectra); spectral tooling `numpy`/`scipy` (Savitzky–Golay), `pybaselines`, `spectralpython`/`PySptools` for hyperspectral cubes; OpenCV/Pillow for smartphone RGB/HSV + colour-card correction; PyTorch Mobile / TFLite / ONNX for on-device inference. Real-world grounding: **FSSAI DART** (India's free manual of 50+ household adulteration tests — an excellent labeling protocol), AOAC official methods, the EU Honey Directive (2001/110/EC), Codex, and ISO 3632 for saffron.

## 1. SAFFRON ADULTERATION

Saffron (*Crocus sativus* L.) is the most economically-motivated-adulteration-prone spice on earth — cut with safflower, turmeric, marigold/calendula, dyed corn silk, *Crocus* stamens/styles, Gardenia, and synthetic dyes (tartrazine, Allura Red). Prior ML work is **mature on the method side** (CNNs, PLS-DA, SVM routinely hit 95–99%+) but **the open-data ecosystem is genuinely thin**: nearly every high-accuracy paper trains on a *self-collected, author-held* dataset released only "on request." The novelty headroom is therefore in (a) cross-domain/cross-device generalization, (b) modality bridging (cheap sensor → lab-grade label), and (c) low-level quantification.

**Verified data landscape (read before the ideas):**
- **VERIFIED-OPEN — SpiceSpectrum** (Mendeley `5v7w2hx8n5`): 11,000 JPG images, 512×512, 11 classes incl. **Saffron, Paprika, Turmeric** (1,000/class), varied lighting/backgrounds. Data-in-Brief paper (PMC12514465). The single best free, citable image asset for saffron-vs-lookalike work.
- **VERIFIED-OPEN — Indonesian Spices** (Kaggle `albertnathaniel12/indonesian-spices-dataset`): ~210 saffron images among 30 spices — a genuinely *different* domain (good cross-domain test).
- **VERIFIED-OPEN — Crocus genomics:** WGS reads + draft genome at NCBI SRA `PRJNA734464`/`PRJNA739096`; GBS alignments on Dryad. Botanical, not an adulteration corpus.
- **NOT OPEN (author-held / "on request") — flag honestly:** Foldscope/Frontiers study (shares only illustrative images), Saff-Kash/SaffNet, the Iran Vis-NIR set (Foods 12(11):2192), HS-GC-IMS, the smartphone-video PLS study, and essentially all FTIR/Raman/NIR-HSI chemometric data. **No public saffron-*adulteration* spectral or HSI dataset was found.**

### 1.1 Cross-device robust saffron-vs-lookalike detector with domain generalization ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Kashmir / Pampore, India (GI-tagged "Kashmir saffron"; heavy counterfeiting).
- **Problem:** Existing classifiers report ~98–99.5% but train and test on a *single* collection (one camera, one country); they collapse on photos from a different phone/market — the exact deployment case for a consumer/vendor app.
- **Novelty / gap:** All prior work (Foldscope-Frontiers; SaffNet; the 2025 MDPI ensemble, Foods 15(10):1661) optimizes *in-distribution* accuracy. The unexplored gap is **cross-domain generalization** — train on one source, test on a held-out *different* source. The two open datasets enable a real Leave-One-Domain-Out protocol nobody has published for saffron.
- **Approach:** ConvNeXt-Tiny/EfficientNet backbone + domain-generalization (strong colour/lighting augmentation, style randomization, DANN-style adversarial or test-time adaptation). Saffron vs {safflower, turmeric, paprika, marigold}; report in-domain vs cross-domain deltas; Grad-CAM (stigma shape vs colour).
- **Datasets:** SpiceSpectrum (verified-open); Indonesian Spices (verified-open, out-of-domain test); optional 50–100 self-collected Kashmir market phone images as a true field test.
- **Feasibility & risks:** High — both datasets free now. **Soft spot:** these are whole-spice/lookalike images, not fractional powder mixtures → scope claims to lookalike/species detection, add a small self-collected mixture set for quantification.

### 1.2 Smartphone "poor-man's spectrometer": adulteration % from screen-illuminated video (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Iran, Khorasan (Mashhad/Torbat-e Heydariyeh — largest producer; loose powdered saffron easily cut).
- **Problem:** Lab methods (NIR-HSI, FTIR, HPLC) quantify accurately but cost €20k+; consumers need a quantitative purity estimate from a phone.
- **Novelty / gap:** A 2024 study (J. Food Comp. Anal. `S0889157524007257`) showed smartphone *video* with a colour-changing screen as illumination gives "spectral-like" data, PLS R²≈0.977 — **but its data isn't released and it used classical PLS only.** The gap: reproduce with an *openly-published* dataset and replace PLS with 1D-CNN/temporal models + uncertainty. No open dataset of this modality exists.
- **Approach:** Videos of saffron powder spiked with safflower/turmeric/dyed-starch at 0–50% (5% steps) under sequential screen colours → per-channel pseudo-spectrum → 1D-CNN / gradient-boosting regression for **% adulteration** with conformal-prediction intervals.
- **Datasets:** **Self-collected (primary)** — this is honestly a data-creation project (its dataset is the contribution; release on Zenodo). SpiceSpectrum colour statistics bootstrap the prior.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Cheap, proven-in-principle. **Soft spot — the data-thin idea:** the whole dataset must be built; normalize with a white-reference frame for cross-phone variation.

### 1.3 Multimodal RGB-image + DNA-barcode species authentication ⭐ (novelty 4–5)
- **Region:** India (Kashmir) + Greek/Iranian provenance — framed as a customs/regulatory-lab tool.
- **Problem:** Images tell you something *looks* like saffron; only DNA tells you *which species* a finely-milled powder actually contains. Nothing fuses the cheap visual screen with a molecular confirmation.
- **Novelty / gap:** DNA barcoding for saffron adulterants is established (ITS2, rbcL, matK; one study caught 20 adulterant species) but it's wet-lab/phylogenetics — **DNA barcoding + ML + image fusion has not been done for saffron.**
- **Approach:** Two-stream late-fusion — (1) transfer-learned CNN image triage on SpiceSpectrum; (2) sequence classifier (k-mer + gradient boosting or small transformer) on reference barcodes for *C. sativus* vs adulterant genera (*Carthamus, Curcuma, Calendula, Hemerocallis, Daucus, Gardenia*).
- **Datasets:** **Sequence stream verified-open** — GenBank/Nucleotide + BOLD Systems barcodes; genome SRA `PRJNA734464`/`PRJNA739096`. **Image stream verified-open** — SpiceSpectrum + Indonesian Spices.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Both streams use free public data — buildable without a lab. **Soft spots:** modalities aren't from the same physical samples → frame as a decision-support pipeline, not paired multimodal learning; verify per-taxon GenBank/BOLD record counts before fixing the class list. Strongest novelty-to-data-risk ratio of the saffron set.

> Also explored, lower data-solidity: an **open multi-instrument saffron spectroscopy benchmark** (Spain/La Mancha). Method novelty is low and **no open spectral dataset is verified** — feasible only with university FTIR/NIR access or a successful author data request. Pitch only if lab access exists.

## 2. HONEY ADULTERATION

> **Dataset reality check.** The biggest trap here is the Kaggle **"Predict Purity and Price of Honey"** (`stealthtechnologies`) and its clones — **explicitly SYNTHETIC by the uploader's own description** (fabricated purity target over invented columns). A second synthetic strand uses **CTGAN** to inflate real data. Models on these report fantasy accuracies and generalize to nothing. **Do not build on them — negative-control only.**

### 2.1 C3-syrup (rice) honey-fraud screen where the legal isotope test fails ⭐ (novelty 5)
- **Region:** India (Apis dorsata / multifloral honey; rice-syrup fraud is the documented national scandal).
- **Problem:** The AOAC 998.12 C4-sugar isotope test — the legal global standard — **cannot detect rice or beet syrup** (C3 plants with δ¹³C nearly identical to honey). Indian fraud specifically exploits this. A real, regulatory-relevant blind spot.
- **Novelty / gap:** The Indian δ¹³C database paper (Foods 2025, 14(8):1289; PMC12026366) confirms 94% of authentic Indian honey passes AOAC 998.12 and explicitly states the method misses C3 syrups. Even SCIRA-with-protein-standard only reaches a 7% C4 floor and still misses rice syrup. **Gap: a fast spectroscopic/chemometric screen aimed squarely at C3-syrup, benchmarked against the isotope method's known failure mode** — almost all ML honey papers lump "syrup" together.
- **Approach:** FTIR/NIR + chemometrics (PLS-DA, GA feature selection, SVM) to discriminate honey spiked with **C3 rice/beet syrup specifically** vs C4 vs pure; quantify the minimum detectable C3 % and compare against the 7% isotope floor. Template: the GA-FTIR study (Discover Food 2025, `10.1007/s44187-025-00619-9`).
- **Datasets:** **VERIFIED-OPEN — Mendeley `tt6pp6pbpk`** (Luo, elemental/ICP-OES profiling): 67 honeys, 17 syrups (incl. C3 & C4), 61 adulterated; tabular ~12 elements (underpins the RF study at 98.4% CV). **Real-but-in-paper** — Indian δ¹³C reference ranges (Foods 2025) must be transcribed; no DOI'd file. **Honest gap:** no large open *FTIR/NIR-of-Indian-rice-syrup-honey* set exists.
- **Feasibility & risks:** High novelty, real regulatory hook. **The ideal Indian FTIR dataset doesn't exist openly** → do C3-vs-C4 discrimination on the Mendeley elemental set (+ any open vibrational set containing beet/rice syrup), framing India as the motivating problem; or treat small own-lab FTIR collection as the contribution. Never substitute the synthetic Kaggle set.

### 2.2 One-class / open-set authentication of EU honey from MIR (model the genuine, reject the fake) ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Ireland / EU artisanal honey.
- **Problem:** Real fraud is open-ended — you can't enumerate every adulterant in advance. Supervised classifiers trained on "pure vs these 5 syrups" silently fail on a novel adulterant. Authentication should be **anomaly detection against authentic honey**.
- **Novelty / gap:** The literature overwhelmingly uses supervised PLS-DA/SVM with known adulterant classes. Gap: rigorous **one-class / open-set** authentication (DD-SIMCA, one-class SVM, deep autoencoder reconstruction error) trained *only on authentic spectra*, evaluated on held-out *unseen* adulterant types — the realistic deployment scenario.
- **Approach:** DD-SIMCA + convolutional autoencoder on authentic spectra only; threshold on reconstruction/Mahalanobis distance; evaluate with each adulterant class held out entirely vs a supervised baseline that never saw it.
- **Datasets:** **Real-but-access-to-confirm** — Irish artisanal honey FT-MIR (~530 samples, incl. dextrose syrup + beet sucrose; verify exact repository/DOI in the source paper's data statement). Secondary: Mendeley elemental set `tt6pp6pbpk` (verified-open) for a fusion extension.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Method-novel, modest data needs (one-class tolerates imbalance). Risk: the Irish MIR set's precise open download needs confirming — if request-only, fall back to any open vibrational honey set and keep the open-set methodology as the contribution.

### 2.3 Cross-batch robust syrup detection in Mānuka honey via hyperspectral imaging ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** New Zealand (Mānuka — the world's most fraud-targeted premium honey).
- **Problem:** HSI models report 95–99% but train/test on the same capture session/instrument, collapsing the moment lighting/batch/sensor changes (classic chemometrics "model transfer" failure).
- **Novelty / gap:** Phillips & Abdulla (Eur. Food Res. Technol. 2022, `10.1007/s00217-022-04113-9`) and the 2025 re-analysis (arXiv:2507.23416) report high in-distribution accuracy. The gap nobody owns: **domain-shift / cross-batch robustness** — train on one product/concentration set, test on held-out brands/sessions, quantify the accuracy cliff.
- **Approach:** 1D-CNN / spectral transformer on per-pixel spectra with **leave-one-brand-out & leave-one-session-out** evaluation; SNV + EPO or CORAL/adversarial domain adaptation; report degradation vs naïve RF/SVM. Deliverable is a robustness curve, not one accuracy number.
- **Datasets:** **VERIFIED-OPEN — University of Auckland figshare** "Hyperspectral Imaging adulterated honey dataset" (`10.17608/k6.auckland.16441686.v1`): Mānuka + NZ honey adulterated with sugar at 5/10/25/50%, real HSI cubes. Plus the companion "Hyperspectral Imaging Honey Database" (12 products / 7 brands / 11 botanical labels) for the cross-brand split.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Strong — data real, sizeable, open. **Soft spot:** only one lab/sensor exists publicly → true cross-instrument transfer can only be *simulated* (cross-session/brand splits, illumination perturbation); be explicit it's a lower bound. Use VAE-generated cubes only as an ablation.

> Also explored: **AI melissopalynology** — pollen-image CNN for botanical/geographic origin (Mediterranean/E-Europe). Verified-open: Cretan Pollen Dataset (Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.4756360`, CC-BY), POLLEN73S/POLEN23E/POLLEN13K. Honey-specific pollen sets are real-but-access-to-confirm. And a **<$50 BME688 e-nose honey screen** (npj Sci. Food 2025) — cheap enough for cooperatives, but the published data's availability must be confirmed; its value is a *real-vs-CTGAN-synthetic honesty audit*.

## 3. MILK & DAIRY ADULTERATION

> **Dataset reality check.** The famous Kaggle **"Milk Quality Prediction"** set (`cpluzshrijayan/milkquality`, 1,059 rows: pH, Temp, Taste, Odor, Fat, Turbidity, Colour → Grade) is **real-as-a-file but scientifically weak** — boolean Taste/Odor, subjective Grade, duplicate rows, no provenance, no instrument, **no adulterant ground truth.** It is *not* an adulteration dataset; treat as a toy benchmark only.

### 3.1 Cross-instrument transfer learning for FTIR milk-adulterant detection (benchtop → portable) ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Generalizable; anchor to Brazil/India cooperative labs with one benchtop FTIR wanting cheap portable field units.
- **Problem:** Models trained on one FTIR don't transfer to a cheaper spectrometer (peak shift, resolution, baseline), forcing costly per-device recalibration.
- **Novelty / gap:** Asseiss Neto et al. (BioData Mining 2019) and Heliyon 2024 report ~98% **on a single instrument**. **Calibration/instrument transfer (domain adaptation) for milk FTIR is largely unaddressed** — the classic chemometrics "calibration transfer" problem with modern domain adaptation.
- **Approach:** 1D-CNN on raw spectra; then **DANN / CORAL / fine-tuning** across instruments (or FT-NIR vs Micro-NIR); few-shot calibration with a handful of shared standards; benchmark transfer-accuracy drop with/without adaptation.
- **Datasets:** **Real, partially-open** — Asseiss Neto 2019 (`10.1186/s13040-019-0200-5`): 4,846 samples, 2,376 adulterated (sucrose/starch/bicarbonate/H₂O₂/formaldehyde) — ⚠️ **only a *sample* is in Additional file 1; full set NOT openly deposited** (request from authors). **Near-ideal paired set:** coconut-milk FT-NIR + Micro-NIR of the *same* samples (PMC11014270, 900+900 spectra). **VERIFIED-OPEN —** Mendeley `byww6nrp2z` (coconut-milk FTIR water adulteration, 43 samples, CSV) as a transfer-target pilot.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Strong method novelty, modest compute. **Biggest risk is data access** — the best bovine set is only partially public; cleanest paired cross-instrument data is coconut milk. Frame the contribution as the transfer method; do author outreach early.

### 3.2 Coffee-ring / evaporative-deposit smartphone screening for village collection centres ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Indian dairy belt — UP, Punjab, Gujarat, Maharashtra cooperative collection centres (lab FTIR unavailable, smartphones ubiquitous).
- **Problem:** Centres pay by fat/SNF and can't screen for water, urea, ammonium sulfate, detergent, starch, or oil at intake; lactometers miss "balanced" synthetic adulteration.
- **Novelty / gap:** Most ML milk work needs benchtop FTIR/NIR/Raman. The **evaporative-deposit / coffee-ring CNN** route (drying a droplet → adulterant-specific crystallization, photographed by phone) is recent and far less explored. Gap: published work does single adulterants at fixed dilution; **multi-adulterant + concentration regression + cross-phone domain shift** is open.
- **Approach:** MobileNet/EfficientNet (quantized, on-device) for adulterant type + multi-task concentration regression; strong domain randomization + TTA for cross-phone/lighting robustness; Grad-CAM mapping decisions to physical deposit regions; interpretable histogram+RF baseline.
- **Datasets:** **Real, open-access papers (verify SI completeness)** — ACS Omega 2024 (`10.1021/acsomega.4c01274`, PMC11209888) evaporative-deposit images for ammonium sulfate/urea/vegetable oil; ACS Omega 2021 companion (PMC8529649); J. Food Sci. 2026 (`10.1111/1750-3841.70831`) image-histogram baseline.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Hardware = a phone + glass slide → highly feasible. **Main risk: you'll likely generate your own image set** (public deposit-image sets are small / partly figure-only) — budget lab time for 1,000+ droplets across ≥2 phones. Cross-phone generalization is the real contribution.

### 3.3 Raman/MIR species-fraud channel-reduction for a low-cost optical sensor (novelty 4)
- **Region:** South Asia / Middle East — premium **buffalo/goat/camel** milk cut with cheaper cow milk.
- **Problem:** Species substitution is invisible to fat/SNF tests; full Raman/FTIR is lab-bound. Can detection reduce to a few wavenumbers cheap enough for a filter-based sensor?
- **Novelty / gap:** Recent work (Food Chem./LWT 2025 Raman; PMC10606090 MIR buffalo/goat/camel) does species ID + quantification with full spectra. **Gap: feature-selection-driven *channel reduction*** — the minimal biomarker set preserving accuracy so a few-band device becomes viable; plus joint species-ID + cow-fraction regression in one model.
- **Approach:** PLS-DA/RF + sparse feature selection (LASSO, RFE, SHAP) → minimal wavenumber subset; multi-task species classifier + cow-% regressor; report accuracy-vs-#channels curve.
- **Datasets:** **Real, access varies** — LWT/Food Chem. 2025 Raman (`S0023643825015245`, check the article's Mendeley Datasets tab); MIR buffalo/goat/camel (PMC10606090); FTIR bovine-breed (PMC11083570). ⚠️ **Many are "available on request"** — verify a downloadable file before committing; channel-reduction analysis works on whatever spectral CSV you obtain.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Clear method novelty. **Soft spot:** spectral species datasets are frequently not fully deposited — confirm a download first or plan collection.

> Also explored, lower data-solidity: **e-nose / flash-GC detection of vegetable-oil/animal-fat in ghee** (India). Real but the **least openly available** category (small, request-only sets) → best as a hardware-partnered collection project; drop for 3.1–3.3 if no e-nose access.

## 4. SPICES (TURMERIC, CHILI, PEPPER & OTHERS)

> Across this domain, **RGB-image datasets for red-chili powder adulteration are the only ones verified as genuinely free and directly downloadable** (two Mendeley + a Kaggle mirror). Hyperspectral/NIR/FTIR spice datasets overwhelmingly sit behind paywalled papers with no data-availability download — flagged honestly below.

### 4.1 Cross-adulterant transfer for red-chili powder (train on brick, detect unseen bulkers) ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Indian red-chili belts — Guntur (AP) and Byadgi (Karnataka), where brick-powder, rice-bran and wheat-bran bulking is documented.
- **Problem:** Models are trained and tested on the *same* adulterant (DenseNet-169 hits 99.99% on brick powder). Real markets mix unpredictable bulkers; a brick-powder model is never validated on unseen rice/wheat bran.
- **Novelty / gap:** Prior work (Brar et al. 2024, `10.1111/jfpe.14762`; the DS-WH-1 XAI 2D-CNN study, `10.1039/D5FB00118H`) reports near-perfect within-adulterant accuracy but **no cross-adulterant generalization study exists.** A leave-one-adulterant-out (LOAO) protocol + open-set "unknown adulterant" detector is a genuine gap.
- **Approach:** EfficientNet/DenseNet + one-class/open-set head (OpenMax or energy-based OOD) under LOAO split; adulterant-% quantification regressor; Grad-CAM to check features are "non-chili-texture," not adulterant-specific.
- **Datasets:** **VERIFIED-OPEN —** DS-WH-1 (Mendeley `mszn5hk9nv`, ~1,638 images, 5 natural adulterants + 16-class 5/10/15% set); brick-powder set (Mendeley `cpm7y44746`, 12 proportions 0–50%+100%), mirrored on Kaggle (`warcoder/adulterated-red-chilli-powder-dataset`). Different adulterant families from different setups — ideal for cross-adulterant/cross-domain testing.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Strong — all anchor data verified free. Domain shift between the two setups is the research contribution, not a blocker. Can't detect colorant *dyes* from RGB → scope to bulking/texture adulterants.

### 4.2 Smartphone RGB + curcumin-colorimetry fusion for turmeric quality & metanil-yellow screening (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Erode and Nizamabad turmeric belts (TN/Telangana), where low-curcumin powder is dyed with metanil yellow/tartrazine.
- **Problem:** Two coupled frauds — genuinely low curcumin, plus banned azo dye added to mask it. A field tool should flag *both* without lab gear.
- **Novelty / gap:** Smartphone curcumin quantification exists (PMID 38607598); CNN+chemometric NIR+RGB fusion exists (Talwar et al. 2025, `S0889157525001383`). **Missing: a single RGB-only multi-task model outputting curcumin grade AND dye-presence probability** as a screening flag.
- **Approach:** Multi-task CNN (shared backbone, regression head for curcumin %, binary head for added dye); printed colour-reference card for white-balance; optional alkaline paper-spot step (curcumin → red-brown, metanil yellow stays bright).
- **Datasets:** **No verified free turmeric RGB-dye dataset** — the cited "turmeric + tartrazine multispectral" set (MTAP 2022, `10.1007/s11042-022-12392-3`) is described as "online" but **no public URL/DOI verified.** Plan **self-collection** (turmeric + 0–1% metanil/tartrazine spikes, curcumin via cheap colorimetric kit, fixed-lighting phone photos) — realistic and cheap.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Sound and cheap; **the student must build the dataset.** Curcumin ground truth without HPLC is approximate (use a standardized kit); dye detection from RGB alone is a *screening* claim, frame honestly.

### 4.3 Black-pepper × papaya-seed adulteration: open-set CV with grain-level quantification (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Idukki/Wayanad (Kerala) and Karnataka — premium-pepper origin where dried papaya seeds (visually near-identical, far cheaper) are the classic bulking fraud.
- **Problem:** Papaya seeds mimic peppercorn size/shape (humans score ~0.97). An automated grader that *quantifies* adulteration fraction in a heap is valuable at collection/export points.
- **Novelty / gap:** A Siamese-network approach exists (Fatima et al. 2022, `10.1111/jfpp.16043`) and HSI "PiperNet" (2026, `10.1080/19440049.2025.2598389`). **Gap: instance-level detection/counting to estimate *adulteration %* from one tray photo, plus open-set robustness to unseen foreign matter** (stems, mineral-oil-coated dull berries).
- **Approach:** YOLO/Detectron instance detector classifying each grain (pepper/papaya/foreign) → fraction estimate; one-class anomaly branch for unseen contaminants; optional gloss cue for mineral-oil coating.
- **Datasets:** **No verified public peppercorn-vs-papaya image dataset** (both anchor papers use custom, undeposited data). **Self-collection very feasible** (cheap materials, RGB only) — the cost is annotation, not access; well-suited to an internship (semi-automated labeling).
- **Feasibility & risks:** Excellent on materials/hardware; work is in grain-level annotation. Mineral-oil detection from RGB is uncertain → stretch goal, not a core claim.

> Also explored, hardware-dependent: **lightweight hyperspectral band-selection for Sudan/Allura-Red dye detection in chili/paprika** (Kashmir/Telangana). Scientifically high-value (≤10-band deployable sensor, LOD curves to 0.1%) but **no public chili/paprika HSI dataset verified** and HSI cameras are costly — recommend only if the host lab already owns a Vis-NIR/SWIR rig.

## 5. EDIBLE OILS & STAPLE FOODS

### 5.1 Argemone-in-mustard-oil low-cost fluorescence quantifier ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** North/East India (UP, Bihar, West Bengal) — loose unbranded mustard oil; site of recurrent epidemic-dropsy outbreaks from *Argemone mexicana* seed oil.
- **Problem:** Argemone adulteration causes epidemic dropsy and glaucoma via sanguinarine. Field detection still uses crude nitric-acid spot tests that miss low-level (<2–5%) contamination.
- **Novelty / gap:** Chemistry is solvable — NMR+PCA detects argemone to ~2% (Food Control 2023, `S0956713523001731`); fluorescence + XGBoost/CatBoost/RF quantifies it (Food Addit. Contam. A 2024, `10.1080/19440049.2023.2297869`). **Gap: a low-cost LED-fluorescence quantifier for the field-screening setting, handling the lab-spike → real-loose-market domain shift.** Calibration transfer + uncertainty-aware regression is the open contribution.
- **Approach:** Fluorescence-spectra regression (gradient-boosted trees + 1D-CNN) with conformal-prediction intervals; CORAL/fine-tuning from lab-spiked to field samples; SHAP on emission bands.
- **Datasets:** **Real, access-to-confirm** — fluorescence spectra of argemone-adulterated mustard/canola from `10.1080/19440049.2023.2297869` (open status unconfirmed — likely author request). The modality (UV-excited fluorescence) is cheap to reproduce in a university lab to extend any seed dataset.
- **Feasibility & risks:** The exact spectral file is the soft spot (likely request-only); cheap reproducibility makes this a genuine build, not pure re-analysis. Field-vs-lab gap is the research interest.

### 5.2 Olive-oil grade & adulteration via cheap fluorescence vs FTIR (calibration-transfer / distillation) ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Mediterranean import chain (EVOO sold in India/Gulf), where lower-grade/seed-oil-cut "olive oil" is common at retail.
- **Problem:** Distinguishing genuine EVOO from refined/lampante and from oils cut with hazelnut/pomace/seed oil — without GC-MS-grade instruments.
- **Novelty / gap:** Olive-oil authentication is the most data-rich corner of this field; PLS-DA on HSI/FTIR/Raman already hits 96–100% (PMC9914562). The open question is **cross-modality transfer** — can a model trained on expensive FTIR be distilled to drive a cheap LED-fluorescence reader? Almost no public work pairs the two modalities on the same authenticated oils.
- **Approach:** Train PLS-DA/1D-CNN/RF on FTIR for grade + adulterant ID; use it as a *teacher* to a *student* fluorescence model (knowledge distillation); report accuracy retained at ~10× lower instrument cost.
- **Datasets (all real):** **VERIFIED-OPEN —** Tapp/Kemsley FTIR olive oil (120 MIR-ATR spectra, 60 EVOOs; `FTIR_Spectra_olive_oils.csv`, Quadram mirror); olive-oil fluorescence + chemical params (Mendeley `10.17632/thkcz3h6n6`, 960 emission spectra, EVOO/VOO/LOO labels); olive EEM during ageing (MDPI Data 8(5):81); optional GC-IMS origin set (PMC9679686).
- **Feasibility & risks:** Sample counts are *small* (24–60 oils) → enforce **oil-level (group) splits + nested CV** to avoid leakage/over-optimism. The cross-modality transfer angle lifts this above re-running solved benchmarks.

### 5.3 Chicory & filler detection in roasted-ground coffee via portable NIR (novelty 3.5)
- **Region:** South India (TN/Karnataka filter-coffee culture) — undeclared chicory/cereal filler in "pure coffee."
- **Problem:** Quantify chicory, barley, maize and husk fillers with a handheld NIR device for FSSAI-style surveillance.
- **Novelty / gap:** Lab FT-NIR + CNN already quantifies chicory/barley/maize (R²>0.98); autoencoders flag chicory 2.5–30% across roasts. **Gap: porting to low-resolution portable NIR (900–1700 nm) with wavelength selection + multi-adulterant simultaneous quantification** — a deployability study.
- **Approach:** CARS/metaheuristic wavelength selection → SVM/PLS detection + multi-output XGBoost/1D-CNN quantification; benchtop vs simulated-portable resolution comparison.
- **Datasets:** **VERIFIED-OPEN —** coffee FT-NIR large set (Mendeley `10.17632/nz2fr76trm`, PMC12142347) — ⚠️ **quality/sensory, NOT adulteration** → use for transfer/pretraining only. Adulteration-labelled chicory/barley/maize spectra (`S0956713522000093`) are paper-internal / likely author-request. Chicory blending is trivially reproducible (coffee + chicory + a NIR pen) → self-collect dilution series, pretrain on the Mendeley set.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Open data is quality-labelled; the cleanly adulteration-labelled spectra are paper-internal. Chemically the easiest target (chicory is spectrally distinct) → keep novelty in portability + multi-filler quantification.

> Also explored: **basmati-purity grain morphology CV** (India/Pakistan). Verified-open: Rice Image Dataset (75,000 images, 5 varieties incl. Basmati, CC0) + UCI Rice #545 — but these are *single-grain, clean-background*, **not** mixtures. The honest gap: **no public basmati/non-basmati *mixture* dataset** → build a small mixture test set (the construction is the contribution) and frame the work as instance-segmentation counting + lab-to-field robustness, else the result is trivial on clean images.

## Cross-cutting: methods & default stack

Food-adulteration ML clusters around a few recurring **detection modalities**, each pairing a sensor with a modeling family; the choice is mostly a trade-off between lab cost/accuracy and field deployability.

- **Vibrational & molecular spectroscopy (dominant).** NIR, FTIR/MIR (ATR), Raman → a 1D absorbance vector → **chemometrics**: preprocessing (SNV, MSC, Savitzky–Golay derivatives, baseline), PCA exploration, then PLS-DA / SIMCA / LDA classification, PLS-R for adulterant level, increasingly SVM, RF, and **1D-CNNs** ("deep chemometrics"). Binary authentic-vs-adulterated accuracies of 95–100% are routine — and the credibility trap, because they come from small single-instrument sets that don't transfer (calibration transfer is a known hard problem). NMR is the gold-standard fingerprint (honey, EVOO) but expensive and open data is scarce. **LIBS** is the specialist tool for heavy-metal/elemental contamination (lead chromate in turmeric).
- **Hyperspectral / multispectral imaging (HSI/MSI).** Per-pixel spectra → a data cube; strong for **powders and spatially heterogeneous adulteration** (saffron, spices, milk powder). Per-pixel chemometrics (PLS-DA, MCR-ALS unmixing) + image-level CNNs (ResNet/DenseNet/EfficientNet, sometimes 3D-CNN/CNN-LSTM).
- **Smartphone colorimetric / RGB computer vision (the deployment-relevant track).** Most realistic for field/consumer use in India. Either chemical colorimetry (reagent/nanoparticle assay photographed, RGB/HSV extraction + regression) or direct image classification (saffron threads, oil colour, chili powder) with CNN transfer learning. A documented variant uses the phone *screen* as a tunable light source. Cheap, offline-capable, sensitive to ambient light → colour-correction/white-reference cards are standard.
- **E-nose / e-tongue.** Cheap sensor arrays for aroma/taste-driven fraud (ghee, spices, oils). Almost no open datasets — usually self-collected.

**Recommended default stack.** `scikit-learn` (PCA, PLS via `cross_decomposition`, SVM, RF, pipelines, nested CV) + `XGBoost`/`LightGBM`; preprocessing with `numpy`/`scipy` + `pybaselines`; hyperspectral I/O via `spectralpython`/`PySptools`/`HyperSpy` + `xarray`; deep learning in `PyTorch`+`torchvision`; smartphone CV with OpenCV/Pillow + PyTorch Mobile/TFLite/ONNX. **Rigor is non-negotiable given the data realities:** always group/instrument-aware splits, nested cross-validation, an external test set, and confusion matrices — not just headline accuracy.

**Regulatory / standards grounding.** FSSAI **DART** (free manual of 50+ household tests — ideal labeling protocol for an Indian smartphone project); AOAC official methods (validation benchmark); EU Honey Directive 2001/110/EC + JRC NMR honey work; Codex (honey, milk); ISO 3632 (saffron crocin/picrocrocin/safranal grading).

## Cross-cutting: data realities & dataset trust tiers (read before committing)

**The synthetic-data trap (most important).** The most popular "food adulteration" Kaggle sets are not real lab data and must never back a working-detector claim:
- **"Predict Purity and Price of Honey"** (`stealthtechnologies`) + clones — **VERIFIED SYNTHETIC** (uploader's own description). Avoid; negative control only.
- **"Milk Quality" / `milknew.csv`** (`cpluzshrijayan`, 1,059 rows) — subjective quality grading, **not adulteration**, no provenance/instrument. Low credibility for fraud.
- **General rule:** any "food adulteration / honey purity / milk quality" set with a handful of tidy tabular columns and no instrument metadata is almost certainly synthetic or hand-tabulated. Real spectroscopy data looks like hundreds-to-thousands of wavelength columns or image cubes.

**Tier 1 — VERIFIED real & openly downloadable today (anchor your prototype here):**

- **Indonesian Spices** (Kaggle `albertnathaniel12/...`), **Indian Spices** (Mendeley `vg77y9rtjb`). *(saffron, spices)*
- **Red-chili adulteration** — DS-WH-1 (Mendeley `mszn5hk9nv`) + brick-powder (Mendeley `cpm7y44746`, Kaggle mirror `warcoder/...`). *(spices)*
- **Auckland hyperspectral honey** (figshare `10.17608/k6.auckland.16441686.v1` + companion DB). *(honey)*
- **Honey elemental profiling** (Mendeley `tt6pp6pbpk`). *(honey)*

**Tier 3 — SYNTHETIC / avoid for any real claim:** Kaggle honey-purity + clones; any CTGAN-generated honey set; the Kaggle "milk quality" set for adulteration purposes.

**Recurring soft spots (have a mitigation ready):**
- **Labelled adulteration-*mixture* data is consistently smaller, less open, and more lab-spiked** than authentication/quality data. Most credible projects therefore **contribute a small reproducible real/field dataset** and tackle the **lab-to-field domain gap** — which is also where the genuine novelty sits.
- **No large open image dataset exists for most Indian-market scenarios** (phone photos of adulterated saffron/turmeric/oil/milk at scale) → expect to self-collect, grounded in FSSAI DART as the labeling protocol.
- **Spectroscopy / HSI raw data is usually in supplements or "on request,"** not one-click repos; HSI cameras and e-noses are costly → country-scope, transfer-learn, or partner with a lab.
- **The 95–100% literature accuracies are single-instrument, small-n, often without external test sets** → budget for instrument/lighting variation, group-aware splits, and a held-out test set; be skeptical of replicating those numbers in the field.

**Suggested first builds (highest novelty-to-effort, all on verified-open data):** the **cross-device saffron-vs-lookalike detector** (SpiceSpectrum + Indonesian Spices) and the **cross-adulterant red-chili transfer study** (the two Mendeley chili sets) — both stand entirely on Tier-1 data today, both have a genuinely fresh framing (cross-domain / open-set generalization), and neither depends on synthetic data, paywalled spectra, or special hardware. For a higher-novelty, regulation-anchored pitch, the **C3-syrup honey screen** is the standout — but be honest that its ideal Indian FTIR dataset must be built or sourced.
