# Region-Specific Internship / Research Project Ideas
### AI/ML for regional problems, with cross-verified open datasets

Generated by a fan-out of 7 domain research agents (each web-searched and verified that the datasets actually exist and are freely accessible). Every idea below follows the mentor's flavor: **a concrete region → a real local problem → an AI/ML approach → data that genuinely exists.**

How to read the novelty score: 5 = genuinely fresh framing / under-served niche; 3 = method is mature, novelty is in the region/data/application twist. Nothing below scores under 3 — saturated topics (generic locust/FAW prediction, plain Landsat-LST UHI maps, Mekong salinity regression, generic methane plume detection) were deliberately dropped.

**Default tech stack for almost all of these:** Google Earth Engine (GEE) for raster pulls + Python (rasterio, xarray, scikit-learn, XGBoost/LightGBM, PyTorch). Most need *no* paid data and *no* heavy compute beyond a single GPU.

## 1. WATER, GLACIERS, FLOODS & HYDRO-HAZARDS

### 1.1 Supraglacial pond "fill-and-drain" GLOF early-signal detector ⭐ (novelty 5)
- **Region:** Eastern/Central Himalaya — Khumbu (Nepal), Bhutan debris-covered tongues (Tshojo, Ngozumpa, Imja area).
- **Problem:** The headline GLOF threat is big moraine-dammed proglacial lakes, but *supraglacial ponds* on debris-covered tongues rapidly grow, coalesce, then drain catastrophically through englacial conduits — and they are under-monitored. Operational inventories track large open-water lakes, not these.
- **Novelty / gap:** Existing tools (ICIMOD inventories, automated mappers) treat lakes as static annual snapshots of *proglacial* lakes. The fresh angle: a **time-series anomaly detector on small (<0.05 km²) supraglacial ponds** that flags the "rapid fill → sudden disappearance" drainage signature on the historically under-mapped debris surface.
- **Approach:** Dense Sentinel-2 (10 m) time series; U-Net segmentation tuned for small ponds against debris confusion (NDWI + SWIR + CNN); per-pond area time series → Bayesian online change-point / LSTM-autoencoder anomaly flag for abrupt drainage; flag downstream reach via DEM.
- **Datasets:** Sentinel-2 SR Harmonized (GEE `COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED`, 10 m, 2017–); Sentinel-1 GRD SAR (GEE, 10 m, cloud-proof for monsoon gaps); ICIMOD HKH glacial-lake inventory (rds.icimod.org); COP-DEM GLO-30. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** GEE does the heavy lifting. Risks: monsoon cloud (mitigate with SAR), tiny ponds near 10 m limit (PlanetScope optional), sparse drainage ground-truth → frame as a screening tool.

### 1.2 Brahmaputra/Padma riverbank-erosion forecaster ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Middle Brahmaputra (Assam) and Jamuna/Padma (Bangladesh).
- **Problem:** Riverbank erosion destroys homes/farmland yearly; communities need to know *which bank segments erode next monsoon* to relocate/protect. Most work maps past erosion, not forward position.
- **Novelty / gap:** Existing studies map historical erosion or static vulnerability. The angle: **forecast next-year bankline migration distance per reach**, with uncertainty, as a probabilistic erosion-risk strip.
- **Approach:** Sentinel-1 SAR banklines (all-weather) + JRC Global Surface Water validation → per-reach migration history → predictors (curvature, width, bar dynamics, upstream discharge proxy from IMERG/altimetry) → LSTM/TCN (or per-reach gradient boosting) → color-coded erosion-risk buffer with confidence.
- **Datasets:** Sentinel-1 GRD (GEE); JRC Global Surface Water Monthly History (GEE); Sentinel-2 (dry-season bankline); GPM IMERG (discharge proxy); COP-DEM GLO-30. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** SAR banklines in GEE are well established. Braided channels are noisy (centerline extraction needed); keep horizons modest (one season/year).

### 1.3 GRACE groundwater downscaling + recharge-response scorecard ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Indo-Gangetic Plain (Punjab/Haryana/UP) — world's most stressed aquifer belt.
- **Problem:** GRACE reveals severe groundwater loss but at ~300 km — useless at district/block scale where policy acts. Managers also need an early read on whether a good monsoon actually recharged the aquifer.
- **Novelty / gap:** GRACE ML downscaling exists (mostly demonstrated on the Indus). The fresh part: extend high-quality downscaling across the *Indian* belt **and add a per-district recharge-response detector** (how much each monsoon's rainfall converts to storage recovery → "whiplash-resilience" score).
- **Approach:** Target = GRACE/GRACE-FO TWS anomaly; predictors = CHIRPS, GLDAS soil moisture/ET, MODIS LST/NDVI, terrain → XGBoost downscale to ~1 km; subtract GLDAS soil moisture for the groundwater component; per-district regression of post-monsoon storage gain vs CHIRPS → recharge-efficiency score; validate against CGWB wells.
- **Datasets:** GRACE/GRACE-FO Mascon (NASA JPL, PO.DAAC/GEE); CHIRPS (GEE); GLDAS-2.1 (GEE); MODIS LST/NDVI (GEE); CGWB well data (India-WRIS, validation). All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Method proven, all inputs in GEE, real validation. Handle the GRACE-FO 2017–18 gap; canal imports can confound recharge signal.

### 1.4 Rainfall-triggered lahar/debris-flow nowcasting for Philippine volcanoes (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Philippines — Pinatubo, Mayon, Taal drainages.
- **Problem:** Loose volcanic sediment + heavy rain = deadly lahars; warnings are qualitative per typhoon. No open channel-specific nowcast exists despite documented rainfall thresholds (Mayon ~40 mm/≥1.4 h).
- **Novelty / gap:** Global landslide nowcasting (NASA LHASA) targets soil landslides, not volcanic-sediment channels; lahar work is physics-based per volcano. Angle: open rainfall-threshold + susceptibility nowcast scoped to lahar source/channel pixels.
- **Approach:** GPM IMERG NRT rainfall (intensity-duration features vs literature thresholds) + COP-DEM susceptibility (slope, flow accumulation) + Sentinel-2 post-eruption ash/sediment proxy → XGBoost/logistic → daily per-drainage lahar probability; validate on documented events.
- **Datasets:** GPM IMERG (NASA, GES DISC/GEE); Sentinel-2; COP-DEM GLO-30; USGS/PHIVOLCS event records (labels). All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** IMERG ~10 km is coarse for narrow channels (downscale); sparse/imprecise event dates; frame as decision-support (PHIVOLCS is authoritative).

### 1.5 Transboundary GLOF "transparency layer" for ungauged upstream lakes (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Bhutan–India and Nepal/Tibet–India transboundary basins (Punatsangchhu, Koshi).
- **Problem:** Downstream Indian communities sit below lakes across borders where in-situ data isn't shared. No neutral, open, frequently-updated risk picture exists for these specific lakes.
- **Novelty / gap:** Inventories are national and snapshot-based. Angle: an **automated open-data monitoring feed scoped to transboundary lakes upstream of populated valleys** — works without cross-border cooperation; framed around data-injustice.
- **Approach:** ICIMOD/GLIMS inventory to select lakes → Sentinel-2 NDWI + Sentinel-1 SAR area extraction → multi-year growth trend → RF/XGBoost hazard ranking (area/growth, dam type/freeboard from DEM, slope, distance/relief to settlement) calibrated on historical GLOFs (Lugge Tsho 1994, Lemthang 2015, Thame 2024) → ranked watchlist + downstream exposure.
- **Datasets:** ICIMOD HKH inventory; Sentinel-2; Sentinel-1; JRC Global Surface Water; COP-DEM. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** All open, no fieldwork. Few labeled events → use as relative ranking, not absolute probability; don't over-claim "early warning."

## 2. FORESTS, WILDFIRE & BIODIVERSITY

### 2.1 Congo Basin deforestation *frontier predictor* ⭐ (novelty 4 — best label availability)
- **Region:** DRC / Republic of Congo logging-frontier forests.
- **Problem:** RADD/GLAD alerts tell you where forest was *already* cleared; rangers want to know where the frontier advances *next* to intercept rather than chase.
- **Novelty / gap:** RADD/GLAD are mature *detection* products. Angle: turn the historical alert stream into a **short-horizon prediction of next clearing locations** (predictive interception).
- **Approach:** RADD/GLAD alert archive as labels → feature stack (distance to recent alerts, road/river network, slope, prior-year clearing density, distance to settlements) → gradient boosting or ConvLSTM on alert-raster sequences → monthly "predicted frontier" heatmap. Use precision@k for patrol budgets (clearing is rare per cell).
- **Datasets:** RADD alerts (WUR/GFW, GEE + portal, 10 m); GLAD-S2/GLAD-L (UMD/GFW); OpenStreetMap roads/rivers (Geofabrik); COP-DEM. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Abundant labels (rare luxury). Driver drift (new roads/policy); class imbalance needs careful sampling.

### 2.2 Fire–Lantana feedback forecaster ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Western Ghats dry-deciduous belt (Bandipur–Nagarahole–Mudumalai / Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve).
- **Problem:** Documented positive feedback: fire opens canopy → invasive *Lantana camara* recolonizes → raises fuel load → hotter next fire. Managers treat fire risk and invasion mapping separately, so post-fire patches about to flip to Lantana-dominated fire-prone states aren't flagged.
- **Novelty / gap:** Lantana SDM and the fire–Lantana hypothesis both exist, but no open tool *couples* them. Angle: use fire history to **forecast which burned patches become Lantana-invaded within 1–2 seasons**, then feed predicted invasion back as future fuel load.
- **Approach:** Per-pixel stack: FIRMS fire frequency + Sentinel-2 indices/texture + Sentinel-1 + terrain + distance-to-edge/road → XGBoost/LightGBM on GBIF Lantana presence + post-fire pixels → invasion probability → simple cellular fuel-feedback layer → GeoTIFF priority-restoration map.
- **Datasets:** NASA FIRMS VIIRS 375 m; Sentinel-2; Sentinel-1; GBIF Lantana occurrences; SRTM/COP-DEM. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** GEE + tabular GBM. Lantana is understorey (Sentinel-2 detection imperfect) → frame as risk probability; correct GBIF road bias with background sampling.

### 2.3 Chir-pine fire-spread pre-positioning tool (mentor example #2, regionalized) (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Uttarakhand/Himachal Himalaya chir-pine (*Pinus roxburghii*) belt.
- **Problem:** Chir-pine litter makes the lower Himalaya extremely fire-prone each pre-monsoon (severe 2024 fires). Responders need next-day spread *direction* to pre-position crews; most Himalayan work outputs static susceptibility, not dynamic spread.
- **Novelty / gap:** Himalayan susceptibility mapping is saturated; next-day *spread* is benchmarked mainly for the US (Google's Next Day Wildfire Spread). Angle: adapt the NDWS architecture to **steep Himalayan terrain + chir-pine fuel** where slope/wind alignment dominate.
- **Approach:** Image-to-image segmentation (today's fire mask + drivers → tomorrow's). Inputs: FIRMS fire mask, ERA5 wind/temp/humidity, terrain slope/aspect, NDVI fuel, drought index → U-Net (attention), pretrain/transfer from public NDWS dataset → daily probabilistic spread map.
- **Datasets:** Next Day Wildfire Spread (Google Research, Kaggle — transfer/pretrain); NASA FIRMS; ERA5 (CDS/GEE); COP-DEM (slope/aspect); Sentinel-2/MODIS NDVI. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Hardest of this cluster (segmentation + transfer). ERA5 wind ~28 km too coarse for valley winds → downscale with terrain / treat slope-aspect as primary; sparse local spread sequences → NDWS pretraining.

### 2.4 Drought-stress tree-mortality early-warning for African savanna (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Kruger/Limpopo savanna woodlands, South Africa (extendable to miombo edge).
- **Problem:** Drought triggers episodic large-tree (marula, mopane) die-off degrading browser habitat and carbon — managers learn of it only after canopy collapse.
- **Novelty / gap:** Bark-beetle early detection in temperate Europe is well-studied; tropical-savanna *drought*-driven mortality early warning is far less developed. Angle: port the LSTM-autoencoder anomaly approach to savanna drought stress.
- **Approach:** Per-pixel Sentinel-2 NDMI/NDVI/NBR time series → LSTM-autoencoder (or anomaly-vs-seasonal-baseline) → cross-reference CHIRPS rainfall anomaly + LST to separate drought stress from phenology → monthly "emerging stress" hotspot map for ranger ground-truthing.
- **Datasets:** Sentinel-2; CHIRPS (GEE); MODIS LST/ERA5; GBIF (species masking). All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Reuses a known recipe. Distinguishing die-off from deciduous leaf-drop is hard → robust per-pixel baseline; no ground-truth labels → screening aid.

### 2.5 Mangrove-loss → fishery-decline linker (mentor example #1, hardened) (novelty 3–4)
- **Region:** Indian Sundarbans (West Bengal) and/or Godavari–Krishna deltas (Andhra Pradesh).
- **Problem:** Mangroves are fish/prawn nurseries; loss is hypothesized to drive local fishery decline, but managers lack a quantified, place-specific link to justify protection.
- **Novelty / gap:** Lots of work maps Sundarbans mangrove change; most stops at land-cover change and never links to an *independent real catch dataset*. Angle: couple Global Mangrove Watch change to **CMFRI district landings** (1950–present, species-resolved) with a **lagged time-series model** → report effect size and lag ("X% mangrove loss ↔ Y% prawn-catch decline at N-year lag").
- **Approach:** Annual mangrove extent + fragmentation from GMW (fill recent years with a Sentinel-2 classifier) → pair with CMFRI landings for mangrove-dependent taxa → lagged regression / GBM / LSTM with SST + rainfall controls → per-district elasticity + dashboard.
- **Datasets:** Global Mangrove Watch v3 (Zenodo/GEE, 10–25 m, 1996–2020+); CMFRI landings (annual booklets/eprints — **PDF parsing needed**); Sentinel-2; NOAA OISST/ERA5. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Main effort is parsing CMFRI PDFs (Claude Code excels). Catch reflects effort/overfishing too → include CPUE/effort controls; district-vs-estuary spatial mismatch needs careful aggregation. **Soft spot: CMFRI data is state/district-level HTML/PDF, not a clean API.**

## 3. MARINE & COASTAL

### 3.1 Seagrass → blue-carbon → dugong-habitat risk index ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Palk Bay & Gulf of Mannar, Tamil Nadu (India's first dugong conservation reserve).
- **Problem:** Palk Bay holds India's last breeding dugong population, wholly dependent on seagrass degrading from trawling/sedimentation/warming. No recurring automated monitoring tied to blue-carbon loss and dugong habitat.
- **Novelty / gap:** Seagrass has been mapped one-off with commercial imagery/acoustics. Angle: **open-data Sentinel-2 time-series change detection** → blue-carbon-stock-loss estimate + updated dugong-habitat suitability (multi-output decision index).
- **Approach:** Sentinel-2 with sun-glint/water-column correction (Lyzenga/DII) → RF or U-Net seagrass classification across years → change map → × carbon-density coefficients for blue-carbon loss → MaxEnt/SDM with bathymetry/depth for dugong suitability.
- **Datasets:** Sentinel-2; Allen Coral Atlas benthic (seagrass class, baseline/validation); GEBCO bathymetry; OBIS/GBIF dugong-seagrass occurrences. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Classic RS + SDM workflow. Sentinel-2 10 m + turbid monsoon water limit fine discrimination → strong glint/depth correction, clear-season composites.

### 3.2 Seasonal HAB / fish-kill early warning (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Gulf of Mannar and southwest (Kerala) coast.
- **Problem:** Recurrent harmful algal blooms cause fish kills and aquaculture loss, but India has no public lead-time bloom forecast for this stretch; warnings are reactive.
- **Novelty / gap:** HAB ML pipelines exist for Oman, Persian Gulf, Gulf of Mexico, Sabah — not this Indian stretch, and most are same-day detection. Angle: **region-tuned spatiotemporal forecast (3–7 days ahead)**.
- **Approach:** Sentinel-3 OLCI chlorophyll + Copernicus/NOAA SST + ERA5 winds + CMEMS currents → ConvLSTM or causal-informed temporal model → rolling bloom-risk maps + threshold alerts.
- **Datasets:** Sentinel-3 OLCI (Copernicus Marine); NOAA Coral Reef Watch/OISST SST; ERA5; CMEMS currents. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** All API-accessible. Sparse ground-truth bloom labels → use chlorophyll-anomaly proxies + INCOIS/CMFRI event reports; cloud gaps need temporal gap-filling.

### 3.3 Dark-vessel IUU fishing watch for Indian MPAs (novelty 3)
- **Region:** Gulf of Mannar Marine National Park, Andaman MPAs.
- **Problem:** Coastal MPAs face illegal trawling inside no-take zones; many vessels run "dark" (AIS off). Managers lack a low-cost repeatable way to flag incursions for patrols.
- **Novelty / gap:** Dark-vessel detection is mature globally. Angle is the **MPA-boundary-aware incursion analytics layer for specific Indian MPAs** — intersect SAR detections vs AIS to isolate dark vessels *inside* MPA polygons, build recurrence hotspots + patrol-priority score.
- **Approach:** GFW SAR detections + AIS effort (or own YOLO/Faster-RCNN on Sentinel-1) → intersect with WDPA MPA polygons → classify matched vs dark → ST-DBSCAN clustering + recurrence → daily risk score.
- **Datasets:** Sentinel-1 GRD; Global Fishing Watch API/BigQuery; WDPA (Protected Planet). All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** GFW ready detections let you skip training. **Soft spot: GFW SAR density over Indian waters is revisit-limited; small wooden boats are below SAR detectability** → target medium/large vessels.

### 3.4 Pre-emptive coral-bleaching + reef-habitat impact forecaster (novelty 3)
- **Region:** Lakshadweep archipelago (extendable to Andaman & Nicobar).
- **Problem:** Reefs bleached severely (2019 IOD, recurring marine heatwaves). NOAA gives generic outlooks; no Lakshadweep-tuned, reef-pixel-resolved bleaching-severity forecast tied to actual benthic habitat.
- **Novelty / gap:** Bleaching ML predictors and NOAA CRW exist. Angle: **region-tuned severity model + Allen Coral Atlas benthic overlay** → "expected area + habitat type of bleaching" per island (decision product, not an SST index).
- **Approach:** CRW SST/SSTA/DHW/HotSpot + ERA5 wind + bathymetry → XGBoost/temporal severity classifier → overlay on ACA benthic/geomorphic layers → affected coral area + lead time per island.
- **Datasets:** NOAA Coral Reef Watch 5km; Allen Coral Atlas v2 (GEE); ERA5. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Inputs fully open. Scarce in-situ bleaching labels → use published survey records + DHW relationships as weak labels; ACA is a static baseline.

### 3.5 Mangrove-loss-driven fish-stock early warning, Sundarbans (novelty 4)
*(A sharper variant of 2.5, scoped to a spatial-lag predictive model.)*
- **Region:** Indian Sundarbans (Hooghly–Matla estuarine complex).
- **Problem:** Net mangrove loss + SLR-driven erosion threaten nursery habitat; no published model links *which eroding patches* drive *which species' landings declines* at sub-district scale for targeted restoration priority.
- **Novelty / gap:** Component techniques exist but the **explicit predictive mangrove-loss → fishery-decline coupling at zone scale with a 1–3 yr lag** appears unpublished. Angle: rank mangrove parcels by "fishery-value-at-risk."
- **Approach:** Annual mangrove edge-erosion/fragmentation metrics from GMW + Sentinel-2 → join to CMFRI/WB landings → XGBoost or panel spatial-lag/distributed-lag model → SHAP attribution → ranked at-risk map + catch-decline forecast. Control for cyclones (IMD tracks) and effort (GFW).
- **Datasets:** Global Mangrove Watch v3; Sentinel-2; CMFRI landings. All verified (same CMFRI caveat).
- **Feasibility & risks:** Light modeling, open data. Landings only state/district-level → treat as regional attribution, not per-pixel causal.

## 4. AGRICULTURE, SOIL, DROUGHT & FOOD SECURITY

### 4.1 Drought-to-distress: crop-stress × mandi-price fusion ⭐ (novelty 5)
- **Region:** Bundelkhand (UP + MP) — chronic drought, agrarian distress.
- **Problem:** Drought hits yields *and* crashes incomes, but the income blow is invisible to satellite-only drought indices. Distress (migration/loan default) is driven by yield × price, not yield alone.
- **Novelty / gap:** Drought monitoring and mandi-price forecasting exist *separately*. The genuine gap: **fuse remote-sensing crop stress with Agmarknet wholesale prices** into a district-level "agricultural distress early-warning" capturing the compound shock — crossing the RS / agri-economics boundary with both-sides-open data.
- **Approach:** MODIS NDVI/LST → Vegetation Health Index + CHIRPS SPI for crop stress; Agmarknet daily modal prices/arrivals → align time series → GBM/LSTM predicting a composite distress index → monthly district risk dashboard.
- **Datasets:** MODIS MOD13Q1 NDVI/MOD11A2 LST (GEE); CHIRPS (GEE); Agmarknet daily prices (data.gov.in OGD API — **free key, 10-commodity/call limit**; Kaggle mirror for history). All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Very feasible solo. Agmarknet has missing days/mandis; "distress" ground-truth is fuzzy → proxy with known drought years (2015–16, 2019). The fusion itself is the contribution.

### 4.2 Field-level "burn-window" forecasting for Punjab stubble (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Punjab + Haryana rice belt.
- **Problem:** Farmers have only a 10–20 day window between rice harvest and wheat sowing, so they burn residue → Delhi-NCR pollution. Authorities react *after* fires are detected — too late to deploy balers/Happy Seeders/subsidies.
- **Novelty / gap:** Existing work *detects* fires (FIRMS) or maps burnt area. Gap: a **forward per-field forecast** — predict when each paddy field enters its high-burn-risk window weeks ahead, ranked by residue load, so resources pre-position.
- **Approach:** Per-field NDVI/EVI + Sentinel-1 VH backscatter → phenology-curve fit (heading/senescence) → XGBoost on historical harvest dates vs VIIRS first-fire dates → weekly risk map of fields entering the burn window in 1–3 weeks, biomass-weighted.
- **Datasets:** Sentinel-2 (GEE); Sentinel-1 (cloud-proof); VIIRS Active Fire VNP14 (FIRMS, labels); ESA WorldCereal (paddy mask, Zenodo). All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** All GEE, clean FIRMS ground-truth. 10 m blurs sub-hectare plots → work at village/grid-cell level; October cloud handled by S1.

### 4.3 Where Punjab's groundwater crash makes paddy unviable (switch-priority map) (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Central Punjab (Sangrur, Barnala, Moga, Ludhiana).
- **Problem:** Punjab pumps ~70% above sustainable limit; table falls ~1 m/yr from paddy. Crop-switching is the agreed fix but there's no spatial tool saying *which areas* are nearest the tipping point and should switch first.
- **Novelty / gap:** Policy argues for switching in aggregate; RS maps paddy. Missing: a fused **"switch-priority index"** (GRACE depletion × paddy extent × low recharge) + per-zone recovery potential.
- **Approach:** GRACE-FO water-storage trend + CHIRPS anomaly + WorldCereal paddy mask + TerraClimate soil moisture → RF downscaling of GRACE to district scale → composite Switch-Priority Index → ranked choropleth + recovery estimate; validate vs CGWB wells.
- **Datasets:** GRACE-FO Mascon (GEE); CHIRPS; ESA WorldCereal; TerraClimate; CGWB wells (data.gov.in). All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Coarse GRACE is the scientific limit — downscaling is the interesting/novel part; report honestly at district level.

### 4.4 Salinization-velocity / remediation-priority map for the Aral basin (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Amu Darya / Syr Darya irrigated belt (Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kazakhstan).
- **Problem:** ~⅓ of irrigated land is salt-affected and worsening, silently cutting cotton/wheat yields. Managers lack a current field-scale map of salinity *trend* to target scarce leaching/drainage water.
- **Novelty / gap:** Many studies map salinity at one time. Gap: a **multi-year change/trend map** fusing radar+optical salinity indices to rank fields by salinization *velocity* for prioritized remediation.
- **Approach:** Sentinel-2 salinity indices (SI, NDSI) + Sentinel-1 + SoilGrids texture → RF per-pixel salinity per year → Theil-Sen temporal slope → "salinization velocity" map.
- **Datasets:** Sentinel-2; Sentinel-1; SoilGrids 2.0; ESA WorldCereal cropland mask; FAO/HWSD + literature ground points. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** GEE-friendly. **Soft spot: open EC ground points sparse** → use published Aral-basin samples + transfer from arid-China datasets; optical indices are proxies for subsurface salinity (trend framing reduces calibration reliance).

## 5. ENERGY, ATMOSPHERE & CLIMATE ADAPTATION

### 5.1 "Below-the-satellite-floor" mini-grid electrification gap map ⭐ (novelty 5)
- **Region:** Sub-Saharan Africa (Nigeria, Tanzania, Kenya, DRC).
- **Problem:** Electrification programs track progress with night-lights, but VIIRS literally cannot see early-stage mini-grids/LED rural supply (~⅓ of LED emission is outside the sensor band), so official "electrified" maps massively undercount and misallocate funding.
- **Novelty / gap:** Most night-light work *uses* VIIRS to claim electrification; a 2025/26 finding shows it can't detect mini-grids. Angle: **flip VIIRS into a calibration-error layer** — build a detectability-corrected gap map distinguishing electrified-but-invisible vs genuinely unserved settlements.
- **Approach:** Settlement clusters (Google Open Buildings + GHSL pop) → label with known mini-grid sites + VIIRS radiance → XGBoost P(electrified | building density, settlement size, distance-to-grid, radiance) → three-class map: visible-electrified / below-floor-electrified / genuinely-unserved priority.
- **Datasets:** VIIRS Black Marble VNP46A2/A3 (NASA); World Bank "Light Every Night" (AWS, no login); Google Open Buildings; GHSL population; mini-grid registries (ESMAP / Nigeria REA / Tanzania). Mostly verified — **label completeness is the weak link** → scope to 2–3 countries with published registries.
- **Feasibility & risks:** High on data/compute. Mini-grid location DB is patchy → country-scoped.

### 5.2 Gas-flaring-to-power opportunity finder ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Nigeria (Niger Delta); transferable to Iraq.
- **Problem:** Nigeria flares billions of m³ of gas yearly — wasted energy + pollution — while the same communities lack power. No tool ranks flares by "wasted energy × nearby unserved population."
- **Novelty / gap:** VIIRS Nightfire flare detection + a 2024/25 exposure-mapping paper exist. Gap: combine flared-gas energy content with co-located electricity-poverty into a ranked **flare-to-power siting score** + growth-trend flag.
- **Approach:** Parse VNF per flare (temperature/radiant heat → flared volume → MWh-equivalent) → buffer + intersect with GHSL population & Black Marble radiance for nearby unserved people → composite score → DBSCAN clustering into sites + trend → ranked siting map + per-site fact sheet.
- **Datasets:** VIIRS Nightfire VNF V4.0 (EOG — **full access needs approval, interim ezCSV usable**); Global Gas Flaring annual volumes (EOG/World Bank); GHSL population; Black Marble; Nigerian Gas Flare Tracker (NOSDRA, validation). All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** VNF is tabular CSV — easy. Volume-to-energy is an estimate but EOG publishes the methodology.

### 5.3 Methane super-emitter monitor for Indian landfills (TROPOMI screen → EMIT confirm) (novelty 4)
- **Region:** India (Ghazipur/Delhi, Deonar/Mumbai, plus Rajasthan/Gujarat/Punjab dumps).
- **Problem:** Urban landfills are major methane point sources (Ghazipur leaked >400 t/h in April 2022), but municipalities have no routine low-cost way to know which dump fired off a super-emission and when.
- **Novelty / gap:** Plume detection exists globally / as one-off studies. Gap: an **operational two-tier per-landfill pipeline** — cheap TROPOMI regional screening flags anomalies → auto-triggers an EMIT 60 m granule search for confirmation + ppm·m quantification → per-site timeline + alert.
- **Approach:** TROPOMI L2 CH4 time series per landfill bbox → z-score anomaly + CNN plume-shape classifier → on flagged dates fetch EMIT L2B plume/enhancement COG → matched-filter confirm + integrate mass → dashboard + email alerts.
- **Datasets:** Sentinel-5P TROPOMI CH4 (GEE); EMIT L2B CH4 Plume Complexes & Enhancement 60 m (NASA LP DAAC); VISIONS EMIT portal (cross-check). All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Analysis-ready COGs/L2. **EMIT is ISS-orbit → revisit sparse/irregular** → confirmation is opportunistic; Sentinel-2/PRISMA optional fallback.

### 5.4 Rapid post-cyclone power-outage & recovery map (novelty 3)
- **Region:** Bay of Bengal cyclone belt — Odisha, Andhra, West Bengal, coastal Bangladesh.
- **Problem:** After landfall (Fani left ~3.5M households dark), responders need fast spatial knowledge of which areas lost power and recovery progress to route crews — official reports are slow/coarse.
- **Novelty / gap:** Black Marble has been used qualitatively for outages. Gap: an **automated reproducible recovery-tracking pipeline** — baseline vs daily post-storm radiance differencing → per-village outage severity + day-by-day "% recovered" curve, auto-triggered by a cyclone track.
- **Approach:** Pre-storm baseline composite (VNP46A2) → difference daily post-landfall images with QA/moonlight/cloud correction → classify severity + track return → animated recovery maps + per-district restoration curves.
- **Datasets:** VIIRS Black Marble VNP46A2 (NASA / GEE); GHSL population / GADM boundaries; IBTrACS cyclone tracks (NOAA). All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** GEE makes baseline+diff trivial. Persistent post-cyclone cloud gaps data → A2 gap-filling + multi-day stacking.

### 5.5 Snowmelt-driven inflow forecasting for run-of-river Himalayan hydropower (novelty 3)
- **Region:** Teesta (Sikkim), upper Ganga/Bhagirathi (Uttarakhand), Nepal.
- **Problem:** Himalayan hydropower depends on melt timing that climate change is shifting; data-scarce basins lack short-to-seasonal inflow forecasts for generation planning + safety.
- **Novelty / gap:** Glacio-hydrology ML is crowded but mostly leans on in-situ gauges. Angle: a **fully open-data, gauge-free inflow forecaster** for a named plant's catchment with explicit melt-season lead time.
- **Approach:** MODIS/VIIRS fractional snow-cover + ERA5-Land temp/precip/melt + NASA POWER + DEM elevation bands → snow-depletion + degree-day features → LSTM/TCN inflow forecast with uncertainty.
- **Datasets:** MODIS Snow Cover MOD10A1 (NSIDC); ERA5-Land (CDS); NASA POWER; COP-DEM; CWC/GRDC discharge (partial, validation). Mostly verified — **open observed discharge is the weak point** → validate vs ERA5-Land runoff proxy or scope to a basin with a public gauge.

## 6. CLIMATE-LINKED PUBLIC HEALTH

### 6.1 EpiClim multi-disease climate early-warning ⭐ (novelty 4 — brand-new dataset)
- **Region:** India, district-level (monsoon-belt states: Kerala, West Bengal, Assam, Maharashtra).
- **Problem:** Dengue, malaria, cholera, acute diarrhoea all spike with the monsoon, but district officers react after cases appear. India lacks an open operational climate-to-outbreak early-warning at district granularity.
- **Novelty / gap:** **EpiClim (released Jan 2025)** already harmonized IDSP weekly outbreak data with climate variables at district level — yet almost nothing has been built on top of it. Angle: be one of the first to turn it into a *predictive* model, not descriptive analysis.
- **Approach:** EpiClim weekly district records (cases/deaths + precip, LAI, temp) as labeled base + CHIRPS + MODIS LST lagged features → per-disease GBM or multi-task LSTM sharing climate signal → next-2–4-week outbreak probability per district → risk dashboard/GeoJSON.
- **Datasets:** EpiClim (Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.14580510); IDSP weekly reports (MoHFW, PDF scraping for updates); MODIS LST. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Hardest input (linked climate-health data) is pre-packaged. IDSP captures *reported* outbreaks → under-reporting bias (be honest); updates need PDF scraping.

### 6.2 Dengue early-warning for Brazil's "new frontier" interior cities (novelty 3, excellent data)
- **Region:** Brazil interior — Acre, Rondônia, Mato Grosso (vs the well-studied Southeast).
- **Problem:** Dengue/chikungunya are pushing into smaller interior municipalities lacking surveillance maturity; local units get hit with little warning.
- **Novelty / gap:** InfoDengue/Mosqlimate already forecast dengue, but winners concentrate on big capitals and treat municipalities uniformly. Angle: a model **specialized for low-incidence, recently-invaded interior municipalities** handling the "first big outbreak" cold-start via transfer-learning from climatically-analogous municipalities; also fold in chikungunya.
- **Approach:** Weekly case counts + temp/precip per municipality from Mosqlimate/InfoDengue API + CHIRPS + ERA5-Land lags → LightGBM or Temporal Fusion Transformer with municipality embeddings → probabilistic (quantile) 1–3-month outbreak-threshold forecasts.
- **Datasets:** InfoDengue/Mosqlimate API (open, free key — **rare case where case data is genuinely open & API-driven**); CHIRPS; ERA5-Land. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** High — documented API with Python examples + worked sprint repos. Interior history is sparse (the cold-start framing is both the hard part and the novelty).

### 6.3 Malaria range-shift risk mapping, East African highlands (novelty 3)
- **Region:** Western Kenya highlands / NW Ethiopia (1,500–2,600 m).
- **Problem:** Is warming pushing malaria-suitable conditions upslope into low-immunity highland populations? Genuinely unsettled scientifically.
- **Novelty / gap:** MAP provides modeled suitability; the highland-warming debate is live. Angle: a **falsifiable, fine-grained suitability-trend model** for specific highland districts + quantify population in newly-suitable altitude bands. The honest "is it really shifting?" framing is the novelty.
- **Approach:** MAP parasite-rate points + CHIRPS + ERA5/MODIS LST + DEM → MaxEnt/GBM suitability classifier → apply across years → suitability trend by elevation band → overlay WorldPop for exposed population, with CIs.
- **Datasets:** Malaria Atlas Project (`malariaAtlas` R package — **cleanest health-data access in the whole set**); CHIRPS/ERA5/MODIS LST; SRTM/COP-DEM; WorldPop. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Clean R/Python tooling. The signal may be weak/ambiguous — but that's a scientifically honest, publishable outcome.

### 6.4 Satellite water-quality precursor for cholera season, coastal Bangladesh (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Coastal Bangladesh (Khulna, Barisal, Chattogram).
- **Problem:** Cholera has a strong spring surge linked to coastal Vibrio blooms; warning comes too late for WASH pre-positioning.
- **Novelty / gap:** Prior NASA/Tufts work showed an autumn satellite "water marker" predicts following-spring cholera — but as papers/internal tools, not an open reproducible pipeline. Angle: **rebuild the precursor end-to-end from open data** and publish a refreshable spring-risk forecast.
- **Approach:** Autumn chlorophyll/ocean-color + SST + coastal precip + river-discharge proxy + GRACE-FO → regularized regression/GBM (small-N, interpretable) with uncertainty → winter-issued spring cholera risk ranking of coastal districts.
- **Datasets:** Sentinel-3 OLCI / MODIS-Aqua ocean color; ERA5/CHIRPS; GRACE-FO. EO inputs verified. **Health outcome data is the bottleneck** — icddr,b / published series only → scope to districts/periods with published incidence; be upfront.

### 6.5 Heat-mortality vulnerability + high-risk-night alerting for an Indian metro (novelty 3)
- **Region:** Ahmedabad (landmark Heat Action Plan) — or Delhi/Nagpur.
- **Problem:** Heatwaves kill unevenly within a city; officials lack neighborhood-level maps to target shelters/water/alerts.
- **Novelty / gap:** HAPs and UHI papers exist but are static snapshots. Angle: fuse **night-time LST** (the variable that drives heat mortality) with vulnerability layers into an operational refreshable intra-city index + short-range "high-risk night" forecast.
- **Approach:** Heat Vulnerability Index from MODIS/Landsat night LST + NDVI + WorldPop + SES proxies (PCA/weighted composite) + ERA5/IMD forecast temp → ward-level vulnerability map + next-3-day high-risk-night overlay.
- **Datasets:** MODIS LST night (MOD11A1); Landsat 8/9 thermal; WorldPop; ERA5 + IMD gridded temperature. Verified. **Ward-level mortality is NOT open** → validate against published Ahmedabad HAP findings; frame as vulnerability/alerting, not death-count prediction.

## 7. AIR QUALITY & URBAN

### 7.1 Lagos intra-city heat-risk index (heat × who's exposed) ⭐ (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Lagos, Nigeria (extendable to Nairobi).
- **Problem:** West African megacities face rising extreme heat with almost no neighborhood-scale heat-risk info; planners need where the hottest places coincide with the most vulnerable people.
- **Novelty / gap:** Plain Landsat-LST UHI maps are common; Nairobi is already block-level via WRI Cool Cities Lab; HE2AT owns Joburg/Abidjan. Defensible wedge: **ECOSTRESS 70 m afternoon-peak LST × building density × age-structured population**, slum-aware, for Lagos (afternoon timing that Landsat's 10:30 overpass misses; most African UHI studies stop at LST).
- **Approach:** Landsat 8/9 LST (baseline/trends) + ECOSTRESS 70 m (afternoon peaks) + Sentinel-2 NDVI/built-up + Google Open Buildings density + WorldPop age structure → transparent composite / PCA-clustering heat-vulnerability index → neighborhood intervention-priority map.
- **Datasets:** Landsat C2 L2 LST; ECOSTRESS L2T LSTE (NASA LP DAAC); Sentinel-2; Google Open Buildings V3; WorldPop age/sex. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** All free, GEE-native. ECOSTRESS revisit is irregular (date-hunting) → keep vulnerability + afternoon-peak fusion as the headline.

### 7.2 Kinshasa informal-settlement growth + heat/flood overlay (novelty 4)
- **Region:** Kinshasa, DRC (deliberately under-studied vs Nairobi/Lagos).
- **Problem:** One of the fastest-growing megacities with massive informal expansion and almost no fine-grained up-to-date settlement maps; planners/humanitarians need where new informal areas emerge and whether they sit in heat/flood-prone zones.
- **Novelty / gap:** Slum mapping is heavily concentrated on Nairobi/Kibera + Indian cities, usually single-date. Angle: **under-mapped city + multi-temporal growth detection + hazard overlay**. (Cross-city transfer from Kibera/Mumbai labels is the literature's stated open problem.)
- **Approach:** Sentinel-2 time series + Google Open Buildings morphology + WSF Evolution/GHSL baseline → U-Net or texture/density classification of informal vs formal → multi-year change detection → overlay new-growth polygons with Landsat LST + DEM → growth map with hazard flags; validate vs GHSL/Open Buildings.
- **Datasets:** Sentinel-2; Google Open Buildings V3; WSF Evolution (DLR); GHSL; Landsat LST + COP-DEM. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** **No Kinshasa-specific informal labels** → transfer/weak supervision (train on labeled cities, validate vs Open Buildings/GHSL) or unsupervised density-texture clustering; informal vs formal is hard at 10 m → lean on Open Buildings morphology.

### 7.3 Stubble-burning → Delhi PM2.5 attribution & lead-forecast (novelty 3)
- **Region:** Indo-Gangetic Plain — Punjab/Haryana burning belt → Delhi-NCR.
- **Problem:** Oct–Nov residue fires drive Delhi spikes but the link is meteorology-dependent (recent work: burning ~14% on average, far less than assumed). Authorities need a 1–3 day forecast of *which* fires will actually hit Delhi to time enforcement/health advisories.
- **Novelty / gap:** Fire-counting (FIRMS) and heavy WRF-Chem CTMs sit at two extremes. Gap: a **lightweight ML pipeline** fusing fires + TROPOMI CO/aerosol + ERA5 wind to predict the fire-attributable *fraction* of Delhi PM2.5 with an interpretable "will this plume reach Delhi" score.
- **Approach:** VIIRS/MODIS fire + TROPOMI CO & UV-AI + ERA5 wind/PBL → trajectory-weighted upwind fire features → XGBoost/temporal model → next-day Delhi PM2.5 + attributable fraction + feature importances.
- **Datasets:** NASA FIRMS; Sentinel-5P TROPOMI CO/AI (GEE); ERA5; CPCB/OpenAQ Delhi PM2.5; MODIS MAIAC AOD. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** All free Python/GEE, bounded seasonal window. Attribution is confounded → decision-support with uncertainty, not a causal claim.

### 7.4 Brick-kiln type-conversion compliance monitoring (SentinelKilnDB) (novelty 3)
- **Region:** Indo-Gangetic Plain — Bangladesh / north India / Pakistan Punjab.
- **Problem:** Brick kilns contribute ~8–14% of IGP PM and use polluting fixed-chimney designs; governments mandate conversion to zigzag but can't verify compliance at scale.
- **Novelty / gap:** Detection is solved (**SentinelKilnDB, NeurIPS 2025, 62,671 labeled kilns**). Gap is not detection but **multi-temporal compliance**: classify FCBK vs zigzag, track conversions/new illegal kilns year-over-year, + population-weighted exposure.
- **Approach:** Sentinel-2 → fine-tune YOLO-OBB on SentinelKilnDB for type classification → run across years for new/removed/converted kilns → overlay WorldPop → compliance dashboard + exposure.
- **Datasets:** SentinelKilnDB (HuggingFace, IIT Gandhinagar); Sentinel-2; WorldPop. All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Pretrained-friendly, ready labels. Labels are 2023-centric → temporal validation + spot-checks; raw detection alone would be unoriginal, so the temporal/compliance framing is essential.

### 7.5 Monsoon-robust street-level PM2.5 nowcasting for Dhaka (novelty 3)
- **Region:** Dhaka, Bangladesh (very sparse regulatory monitors).
- **Problem:** Among the world's most polluted cities yet few reference monitors; satellite AOD is heavily missing during the long monsoon — exactly when gap-filling matters for health messaging.
- **Novelty / gap:** AOD+ML→PM2.5 is established (and already done for Kampala/Nairobi/Dakar). Gap: Dhaka is under-covered + explicit **cloud/monsoon AOD-gap imputation** + per-pixel uncertainty.
- **Approach:** MODIS MAIAC AOD + ERA5 meteorology + VIIRS night-lights + sparse OpenAQ/US-Embassy stations → gap-aware GBM/spatiotemporal model (impute AOD via meteorology + temporal priors when missing) → daily ~1 km PM2.5 with uncertainty; validate vs ACAG SatPM2.5 + held-out stations.
- **Datasets:** MODIS MAIAC AOD; ERA5/ERA5-Land; OpenAQ + AirNow Dhaka; VIIRS Black Marble; ACAG SatPM2.5 (validation). All verified.
- **Feasibility & risks:** Fully free stack + ready validation baseline. Few Dhaka ground points → cross-validation + ACAG; keep monsoon-gap robustness as the core contribution.

## Cross-cutting data realities (read before committing to one)

**Cleanest data (lowest risk to start):** anything built only on GEE rasters — Congo frontier predictor, Brahmaputra erosion, GRACE downscaling, fire–Lantana, supraglacial ponds, Lagos heat, salinization-velocity, mini-grid gap, gas-flaring. These have abundant, ready, free inputs.

**Pre-packaged labeled datasets (huge head-start):** EpiClim (climate-health, India), SentinelKilnDB (brick kilns), Next Day Wildfire Spread (transfer for Himalayan fire spread), Mosqlimate/InfoDengue API (open dengue case data), Malaria Atlas Project R package, Global Fishing Watch (ready vessel detections).

**Known data soft spots (have a mitigation ready):**
- **Health outcome counts** are the hardest category — favor EpiClim, Mosqlimate, MAP, or validate-against-published-series (cholera, heat-mortality).
- **CMFRI fish landings** = state/district PDFs, not an API (parse with Claude Code; treat as regional attribution).
- **GRACE** native ~300 km — downscaling is statistical; validate hard against CGWB wells.
- **ERA5 wind ~28 km** too coarse for mountain valley winds — downscale with terrain or lean on slope/aspect.
- **EMIT/ECOSTRESS** are ISS-orbit → irregular revisit (opportunistic confirmation; have a fallback sensor).
- **Mini-grid / salinity / informal-settlement labels** are patchy — country-scope or use transfer/weak supervision.
- **GFW SAR / small-boat detection** over Indian waters is revisit-limited and misses wooden boats.

**Suggested first build (highest novelty-to-effort):** the Congo deforestation frontier predictor or the Bundelkhand drought-to-distress fusion — both have rich open labels, a genuinely fresh framing, and no health-data or commercial-imagery dependency.
